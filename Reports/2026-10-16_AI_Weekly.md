Local-first tool that indexes a repo and exposes task-oriented MCP tools (get_overview, get_health, get_change_risk, get_dead_code, etc.) so agents in Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or VS Code don't re-discover the codebase each session. v0.57.0 added Change Records reconstructed from git with an evidence checklist, a local policy linter emitting SARIF/JUnit, headless chat with auto-generated charts/tables, opt-in edit-attribution capture, a 'suspects' feature ranking recent changes against a bug symptom, CycloneDX dependency-inventory export, MCP-HTTP server auth with repo/tool scopes, cross-repo queries, and new language support (Astro, Swift, C#/VB.NET, Spring). ## Azure AI & Microsoft

A dense week for Microsoft Partners: Microsoft launched a dedicated decision/classification model in Foundry, GitHub Copilot added Gemini 4 Argon and an upgraded HydraFusion Max router while announcing the deprecation of three models and GA of agentic autofix for code scanning, and Microsoft disclosed deep technical details of its next-gen Maia 300 AI accelerator.

### Microsoft-Decision-1: a dedicated decision/classification model launched in Microsoft Foundry
**Produkt:** Azure AI Foundry<br>**Datum:** 2026-10-09<br>**Relevanz:** 🔴

Microsoft shipped Microsoft-Decision-1, a non-generative model post-trained from Alibaba's Qwen3.5-9B that returns calibrated probabilities over a fixed set of answer options (yes/no, multiple choice, ratings, rubric scoring) instead of free text. Pricing is $0.042 per million input tokens with output free; Microsoft's own example (86 input/2 output tokens for an incident-prioritization call) costs about $0.0000036, i.e. $1 covers roughly 275,000 decisions. Microsoft claims top accuracy across 36 internal benchmarks (~150k held-out questions) and about 2.5x the speed of the runner-up. Use cases: request routing/classification, incident triage, verifying AI-generated outputs, agent-action selection, content labeling. Practical impact for a Microsoft Partner: a materially cheaper, lower-latency building block than calling GPT/Claude for routing, guardrail/verification, and agent-decision steps inside client Foundry solutions - worth evaluating as a cost-optimization layer, but it gives no rationale per decision, so pair with audit logging/human review for high-impact use.

### Microsoft discloses Azure Maia 300 AI accelerator architecture
**Produkt:** Azure AI Services (infrastructure)<br>**Datum:** 2026-10-12<br>**Relevanz:** 🟡

Microsoft published a deep technical dive on Maia 300, its next custom AI accelerator: TSMC N2P compute dies with separate chiplet dies for memory/IO, 8x HBM4 stacks giving 288GB capacity and 18 TB/s bandwidth. A rack packs 96 accelerators in a 167kW envelope (2x Maia 200 density) with liquid cooling removing over 80% of thermal load; a two-tier Ethernet scale-up network links up to 128 racks (up to 12,288 accelerators per superpod). Microsoft claims up to 7x rack-scale token throughput and over 2.5x performance/watt vs. Maia 200, targeting agentic/long-context/reasoning-heavy inference. No GA date was given - more detail comes at Ignite (Nov 17-20, 2026); capacity will flow indirectly into Copilot, GitHub Copilot and Microsoft Foundry. Practical impact: signals future capacity/cost/throughput improvements for Foundry-hosted and Copilot workloads worth tracking ahead of Ignite, though nothing actionable or purchasable yet.

### Perplexity open-sources SPACE, the sandbox platform behind Computer
**Datum:** 2026-10-15<br>**Relevanz:** 🔴

Perplexity open-sourced SPACE, the microVM-based sandbox infrastructure that has run all Computer agent sessions since July 2026, handling millions of sandbox creations per day. Each sandbox is an isolated VM with its own OS; a central control plane creates/pauses/resumes/removes workspaces, keeps network access and credentials outside the untrusted sandbox, and supports checkpoint-based snapshots and session branching. Perplexity reports sandbox creation 3-5x faster than its prior commercial provider on the same production traffic, and security testing found zero VM-to-host escapes in 108 attempts. CoreWeave and Akamai have already launched SPACE on their clouds, and Crusoe is adding it to its Managed AI Services. Directly relevant for engineers building/deploying agentic systems needing secure, scalable sandboxing.

[Perplexity Blog - Open-sourcing SPACE](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/open-sourcing-space)

### Arcade.dev launches Tool Recommendation for AI agents
**Datum:** 2026-10-15<br>**Relevanz:** 🔴

Arcade.dev launched Tool Recommendation, an MCP-based system that lets agents query a catalog of 8,000+ tools without loading full tool definitions into context (describing the full catalog would cost ~1.75M tokens). Specialized models search/rank the catalog and return a shortlist; the agent's own model still picks the action to run. Modeled savings of up to 37% on input costs vs. a fixed 100-tool assistant and a measured 7.6% reduction in total input+output token charges across 408 real sessions using Codex and Claude Code, with task completion unchanged. On the ToolRet benchmark, Arcade scored 56.8% Recall@10 on web tools vs. Microsoft Foundry's 46.0%. Included free for Arcade Cloud customers; competes with Anthropic's Tool Search Tool and Microsoft Foundry Toolboxes - directly relevant to consulting teams building cost-efficient enterprise agent stacks.

## Industry & Research

The EU's AI Scientific Panel held an emergency session on frontier-model loss-of-control incidents and recursive self-improvement risk, while Anthropic made three governance moves: giving a nonprofit evaluator employee-level access to pre-release models, publishing election-safety data ahead of the 2026 US midterms, and signing an AI governance MOU with the Singapore government.

- **EU convenes special AI Scientific Panel session on frontier AI 'loss of control' incidents:** On Oct 9, 2026 the European Commission's AI Act Scientific Panel held an unscheduled special meeting, convened by Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen and panel chair Sean O hEigeartaigh, explicitly to inform Commission action on 'recent loss-of-control incidents' and on technical developments reducing frontier-model monitorability and enabling recursive self-improvement. The AI Office has already requested serious-incident reports from the developers involved. The panel chair recommended four steps: governance of internal models used during development/testing, a dedicated EU task force to detect/investigate such incidents, an international information-sharing initiative with common requirements for frontier developers, and more scrutiny of training techniques that reduce model monitorability. No formal enforcement decisions were taken. [European Commission, Digital Strategy news](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/special-meeting-ai-scientific-panel)
- **Anthropic gives nonprofit evaluator METR employee-level access to pre-release models:** On Oct 15, 2026 Anthropic disclosed a new, deepest-access 'Tier 3' arrangement with METR under its independent-evaluators program: a six-person METR team now gets company hardware, office space and internal tools roughly on par with Anthropic's own internal risk-assessment teams, and can publish its own analysis of Anthropic's risk levels/incidents/practices without Anthropic holding veto power (only narrow redactions, e.g. customer data). Separately, SecureBio is reviewing the bio/chem-weapons chapter of Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report under a lower, narrowly-scoped tier, and Anthropic is in early talks with other groups including Hugging Face. [Anthropic - An update on independent evaluators](https://www.anthropic.com/news/an-update-on-independent-evaluators)
- **Anthropic publishes election-safety measures and usage data ahead of 2026 US midterms:** On Oct 14, 2026 Anthropic detailed its midterm-election safeguards. From a privacy-preserving sample of ~250,000 US Claude.ai conversations (Sept 21-27), 5.75% touched politics/policy/social issues and only 0.002% violated election-integrity policy. Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 scored 99% on Anthropic's internal political-evenhandedness evaluation; outside reviewers found Claude rarely refuses political queries but sometimes over-hedges.
